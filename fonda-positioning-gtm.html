01

What Fonda actually is

Read back from the product itself — the four surfaces, the PMS and inbox integrations, and the hotel-profile brain — a much bigger thing than "email" comes into focus.

Today the site leads with the inbox. But the codebase describes an AI operations layer that does four jobs at once and, crucially, learns the specific hotel before doing any of them:

Surface 1

Guest email, drafted

Every incoming message is classified and answered in the hotel's own voice, ready for the GM to review and send.

Surface 2

The morning brief

Arrivals, departures, overnight email, occupancy and the one thing needing a decision — before the desk opens.

Surface 3

Check-in chasing

Missing arrival times are chased automatically so housekeeping and the desk can plan the day.

Surface 4

Ask your hotel

Plain-language answers over live data — occupancy, revenue, who's arriving late — no report-building.

Underneath all four sits the part that makes it defensible: a hotel brain. Fonda ingests the PMS (MEWS / Apaleo) for reservations and occupancy, connects the inbox (Gmail), and stores a rich profile — policies, tone, room types, upsells, local recommendations, reviews. It learns the hotel once and puts it to work everywhere. And it keeps the GM in control: drafts are for review; nothing is sent autonomously.

So the real product is: an AI front-office that runs an independent hotel's repetitive admin — communications, arrivals, the daily brief, and instant answers — on top of the systems the hotel already uses, always under the GM's approval.

"Inbox, answered" is the wedge — the sharpest, most demoable pain. It is not the product. Anchoring the whole brand to it caps how big Fonda is allowed to look, and invites the wrong competitor comparison (a generic email assistant) instead of the right one (a teammate that runs your front office).

Lead with the role, back it with the layer. Position Fonda as "the AI front office for independent hotels" (concrete, salary-anchored, evocative) and use "AI operations layer" as the explanatory descriptor in meta, nav and investor materials. The role sells; the layer explains.

One category boundary matters more than any other: Fonda is not a PMS. Mews and Apaleo are the system of record. Fonda is the intelligent layer that sits on top of them. This is a feature, not a limitation — it's why setup is same-day, why there's nothing to migrate, and why the PMS marketplaces are a distribution channel rather than a competitor. Every message should reinforce "works with your PMS," never "replace your PMS."

Positioning statement

The internal north star every headline, email and deck should ladder up to.

Positioning

For the GM of an independent hotel who is buried in front-office admin and can't justify another hire, Fonda is the AI front office that drafts every guest reply, chases arrival times, and delivers a plan each morning — learning your specific hotel and working on top of the PMS you already run. Unlike hiring more desk staff or bolting on a generic AI, Fonda knows your hotel and keeps every action under your approval.

The one-sentence version

Fonda runs your hotel's front office — so you can run your hotel.

The three things it must always say

  • Scope: it's the whole front-office admin (comms + arrivals + brief + answers), not just email.
  • Specificity: it learns your hotel — tone, policies, rooms, PMS data — so it's not generic AI.
  • Control: drafts for your review; it never acts behind your back. This defuses the #1 hotelier fear.

06

Brand & name leverage

The name is already doing strategic work — lean into it.

A fonda is a traditional Spanish/Catalan inn — a small, family-run place to eat and stay. That's a gift for this positioning: it signals independent, warm, Mediterranean, human — the exact opposite of enterprise hotel software. It quietly tells the buyer "this is built for a place like mine," and it pairs naturally with a garden/seaside hero (see the illustration proposals). Keep the wordmark restrained per Signal, but let the story of the name carry warmth the visual system deliberately withholds.

Tagline options (descriptor slot)

  • Hotel operations, on autopilot. — current; broad, accurate, keep as the utility descriptor.
  • The AI front office for independent hotels. — best for clarity/SEO.
  • Run the front. Keep the soul. — campaign line; captures the independent-hotel emotion.
  • Your hotel, handled. — short, warm, works as a sign-off.
07

Go-to-market

Ideal customer profile

Independent and boutique hotels, roughly 20–200 rooms, run by an owner-operator or a stretched GM who wears every hat. They already run a modern cloud PMS (MEWS or Apaleo) — which both signals sophistication and gives Fonda its integration. Front desk is understaffed; OTA and guest email pile up overnight; the GM is doing admin instead of hospitality and can't justify a full-time night/admin hire. Europe-first is the natural beachhead — MEWS/Apaleo are EU-strong, the product speaks English, Spanish and Catalan, and the design partners are Mediterranean boutiques.

The wedge, then the land-and-expand

Lead with the email assistant. It is the sharpest wedge: highest-frequency pain, instantly demoable on the hotel's own inbox ("connect it, see drafts tomorrow morning"), and visible ROI within a day. The morning brief is the retention hook — a daily habit that makes Fonda part of the routine. Then expand:

Channels, in priority order

  • PMS marketplaces (MEWS Marketplace, Apaleo Store). Highest-intent distribution on earth for this ICP — hoteliers browse these for exactly this. Get listed; it doubles as credibility.
  • Hotel-tech directories & reviews (Hotel Tech Report and similar). Where GMs shortlist. Seed with design-partner reviews.
  • Founder-led outbound. Fonda can demo on a prospect's public inbox — a devastatingly concrete cold email. Personalize per property.
  • Independent / boutique hotelier communities & associations. Regional hotelier groups (Spain/Catalonia first), boutique networks, WhatsApp/LinkedIn owner circles. This ICP is word-of-mouth-driven.
  • Localized content & SEO (EN/ES/CA). "Handling OTA email," "the GM's morning routine," "MEWS + AI." Pairs with fixing the site's thin IA (see audit #16). This is how Mews wins search — Fonda can win the long tail.
  • Referral. Boutique owners talk to each other. A simple "refer a hotel" incentive compounds in a tight community.

Pricing narrative

Keep it flat, per-property — no per-seat, no per-email metering. It fits small teams, is predictable, and lets you anchor against labour: "less than a day of front-desk cover per week." The €199/property/month already in the product is a strong, simple anchor. Beta play: first 20 hotels free as design partners, then convert with a founding-member rate. One decision to make (flagged in the audit): show the price or go "talk to us" — don't do both. For a low-friction, self-serve-ish beta, showing the flat price reduces sales friction and suits the ICP's "just tell me what it costs" temperament.
