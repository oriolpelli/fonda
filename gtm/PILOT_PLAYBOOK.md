# Fondas — Pilot Playbook

_Owner: Oriol · 23 September 2026 · Authority for **what to say and what to send** to a prospect hotel._

**What this file is.** Every script for getting from a stranger's lobby to a signed pilot: walking in, reception, the phone, email, LinkedIn and WhatsApp, the follow-up sequence, the 20-minute demo, objections, the close, the pilot agreement, onboarding, and the day-30 and day-60 conversations. One place, so there is never a second copy of a line to drift.

**What it is not.** *Why* this market and this price is `GTM_STRATEGY.md`. *What we are* and the voice is `POSITIONING_V3.md`. *When* is `ROADMAP.md` (priority) and `PILOT_SPRINT.md` (the day-by-day of the current fortnight). The 60-second door pitch is `pitch/GUION_60S.md`, used with the five-screen pass [Fondas Sixty Seconds](https://claude.ai/artifact/YWgnwHMNBpUEYQx4sgySkj) and the one-pagers in `pitch/`.

**Replaces:** `GTM_STRATEGY.md` §4.7–4.13 and `POSITIONING_V3.md` §5.1–5.4. Those sections are now pointers here.

Scripts are in Spanish (and Catalan where it matters in person), because the first pilots are Spanish. **Tú by default** — it is how Barcelona hospitality talks. Switch to *usted* the moment they use it, and start with *usted* in a Gran Lujo property with a senior director.

## 0. The approach, on one page

### 0.1 What we sell: the whole operativa, and where it's going

**Never sell "the first hour".** A small hotel hears *la primera hora* and thinks *«eso lo hago yo en un rato»* — it makes Fondas sound like a morning convenience. `POSITIONING_V3.md` §1.3 already rejected it for the site; the pitch follows. Fondas runs the admin **all day, across the whole stay** — before the guest arrives, the day they arrive, while they're in the house, after they leave — and it gets bigger every week.

Sell it in three layers, always in this order:

| Layer | What you say | How you prove it |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Today, all day** | The brief before you open · every guest email, before arrival and in-house, matched to its reservation and drafted in the hotel's voice · arrival times chased · departures · each guest's record, with what they asked last time · any question about the hotel, any time | The demo, on the screen, live |
| **2. What's coming** | Reputation (reviews and how the score moves) · the rate signal in the brief · pre-arrival extras with the hotel's prices · the stay plan for each guest · then housekeeping, F&B, staff, finance | The locked tiles in Home › *Personalizar* — the roadmap is inside the product |
| **3. Why now** | Founding hotels get 60 days free, everything we ship in that time, the founding price for life, and a say in what gets built next | The shipping pace: **20 new product features in the last 30 days.** The guest record, saved conversations, the two communications inboxes and the customisable Home didn't exist a month ago |

**How to talk about what's coming — the line between selling and overpromising.**

- **Present tense only for what's live.** Everything in layer 1 is *hace*. Everything in layer 2 is *viene* / *en camino*.
- **No dates for a specific feature.** Promise the *pace* (*«sacamos mejoras cada semana»* — true, and checkable in their own account), not *«reseñas el 15 de octubre»*. A missed date costs a pilot; a kept pace wins one.
- **They never pay for a promise.** The day-60 decision is about what's on the screen that day. Say it out loud — it's what makes layer 2 believable.
- **Layer 2 matches `ROADMAP.md` and `lib/roadmap.ts`.** If an item isn't there, it doesn't go in the pitch.

The line to remember, and the one to say when a director asks what Fondas *is*:

> **ES:** Fondas lleva la operativa del hotel, todo el día: lo que hoy hace tu recepción entre correos, llegadas y papeleo. Y cada semana hace más.
>
> **CA:** Fondas porta l'operativa de l'hotel, tot el dia: el que avui fa la teva recepció entre correus, arribades i paperassa. I cada setmana en fa més.

### 0.2 Channels

**Channels, in order of what converts for a local founder with no logos.**

| Rank | Channel | Why | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Warm intro** | A referred GM takes the call most of the time. Borrowed trust is the only kind you have yet. | Everyone you know in hotels, F&B, hotel tech, consulting |
| 2 | **Walk-in** | Nobody else selling software to a 30-room boutique will ever stand in its lobby. A face and a printed brief beat any inbox. | The 22 Barcelona hotels in the tracker, and anything drivable |
| 3 | **Phone** | Reaches the director past the inbox; best as the *second* touch after a visit or an email | Day-4 follow-up; Madrid and coastal hotels you cannot visit |
| 4 | **Email + LinkedIn** | Cheap and scalable, weakest alone. Founder-led cold email to a tight ICP runs 8–12% positive replies and 1.5–2.5% meetings ([Cleverly](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/cold-email-strategy-for-b2b-saas)) | The written trail after every visit and call; cold for far-away hotels |

**The ladder.** Every touch asks for exactly one step up, never two:

```
door → director's name + email → sample brief sent → 20-min demo → 30-min setup → pilot live → day-30 "we continue" → day 61, founding price
```

**Every touch leaves something behind.** A one-pager, a printed brief with a handwritten note, an email with the sample link. The director should be able to meet Fondas without you in the room.

**Rules that don't bend.**

1. **Lead with the work, not the technology.** Say *«Fondas lee tus datos y escribe esto»*, not «IA». The word is on the website; it is not how you open a conversation.
2. **Present tense for what's live, future tense for the roadmap, no dates** (§0.1). Sell the trajectory hard — but as a trajectory (`POSITIONING_V3.md` §0.4, §10).
3. **Never knock a competitor or the software they already chose.** The villain is the admin that eats the day, not Apaleo or Opera.
4. **Never promise a PMS connection as live.** MEWS and Apaleo are connected; anything else pilots on the daily-export Sheet source (§8).
5. **Four touches to the director, then stop.** A visit where you only met reception doesn't count; a note or email does. After four, the hotel goes to the November list.
6. **Don't sell to reception.** Reception is your first ally and future user; the director or owner decides.
7. **Say three, aim for four.** Publicly it is *tres hoteles fundadores* (the site says *los tres primeros*). Sign four, because one will go quiet.
8. **No fake proof.** No invented numbers, logos or quotes. *«Estáis entre los primeros»* is a strength when said plainly.

## 1. Before you walk in

### 1.1 Ten minutes of research per hotel, the evening before

Fill these in the tracker (`Fondas_Outreach_Tracker.xlsx`) before you go:

| What | Where to find it |
|---|---|
| **Director's name** | The hotel site's *equipo / nosotros* page; LinkedIn «director» + hotel name; **Google and TripAdvisor review replies** — directors often sign them; press mentions |
| **Who owns the guest inbox** | Guess now, confirm at reception (§2.2) |
| **PMS** | Click *Reservar*: a URL with `mews.com` or `apaleo` confirms it. Anything else is only the booking engine — ask in person |
| **Rooms, restaurant, guest mix** | Hotel site; Booking.com listing |
| **One specific detail** | A recent renovation, an award, a review praising the team. You mention it once, sincerely, or not at all |

### 1.2 The kit

- **The envelope, one per hotel:** the one-pager (`pitch/Fondas_una_pagina_ES.pdf`, and CA/EN on you) + a printed sample brief + a handwritten sticky note: *«[Nombre]: así empieza el día con Fondas — y sigue todo el día. Te escribo esta tarde. — Oriol, [móvil]»*. Handwriting is the point — it is the opposite of spam.
- ⚠️ **Don't hand out the current one-pager PDFs.** They predate the founding offer: they say *30 días gratis*, open with a morning-only headline, and promise three features *«durante el piloto»*. Regenerate them from Appendix A first (`README.md`, known issue 1).
- **Phone:** charged, [Fondas Sixty Seconds](https://claude.ai/artifact/YWgnwHMNBpUEYQx4sgySkj) open in ES, the demo hotel logged in on the dashboard, a hotspot in case the lobby Wi-Fi asks for a room number.
- **A folder, not a laptop bag.** Smart casual — dress like a hotel's own sales manager.
- **Your contact line:** mobile + a `fondas.app` address. An iCloud address on a business card reads as a side project; if `hello@fondas.app` doesn't receive mail yet, fix that first (`GTM_STRATEGY.md` §3.3 flagged it in August).

## 2. In person

### 2.1 When to go

Reception has two rushes: check-outs and billing in the morning, arrivals from mid-afternoon into the evening ([GuruHotel](https://guruhotel.com/blog/hotel-front-desk-guide)). Spanish directors are in before lunch and after it. The windows below follow from that; they are judgement, not a published benchmark — adjust after your first ten visits.

| Window | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Tue–Thu, 11:45–13:30** | **Best.** Check-outs done, arrivals not started, director in the building before lunch |
| Tue–Thu, 16:30–18:00 | Second choice, small hotels only. If there's a queue at the desk, leave and come back |
| 08:00–11:30 | No — check-outs and billing |
| 14:00–16:00 | No — lunch, and early check-ins begin |
| Monday morning, Friday afternoon, weekends | No — weekly meetings, weekend arrivals |
| City festivals and big events | No — **La Mercè, Thu 24 – Sun 27 Sep 2026**: hotels full, directors on the floor |

**Four or five hotels per window**, planned by walking route. Put the tracker's Barcelona hotels on Google Maps and order them by the map:

| Loop | Hotels |
|---|---|
| **A — Ciutat Vella** (10) | Ohla Barcelona · Yurbban Trafalgar · Banys Orientals · The Serras · Mercer · Neri · Denit · The Wittmore · DO Plaça Reial · Casa Camper |
| **B — Eixample** (10) | Praktik Rambla · Sixtytwo · Actual · Casa Bonay · Granvia · The Corner · Constanza · Cram · Villa Emilia · Hotel Market |
| **Singles** (2) | Brummell (Poble Sec) · Primero Primera (Tres Torres) |

The tracker's *Walk-in loop* column carries the same split.

### 2.2 Reception: your first user, not a gatekeeper

The person at the desk does the admin Fondas removes. Treat them as a future user. Open in the language they greet you in: *«Bon dia»* → Catalan.

**The opener**

> **ES:** Hola, buenos días. Soy Oriol, de Fondas, aquí de Barcelona. Trabajo con hoteles de la ciudad y traigo una cosa para el director o la directora — es un minuto. ¿Está por aquí?
>
> **CA:** Hola, bon dia. Sóc l'Oriol, de Fondas, d'aquí Barcelona. Treballo amb hotels de la ciutat i porto una cosa per al director o la directora — és un minut. Hi és?

**The one question to ask reception** — only if the desk is quiet. It tells you who owns the inbox (and so who decides), and gives you a number to use with the director:

> **ES:** Una curiosidad: los correos de los huéspedes, ¿los contestáis vosotros o los lleva dirección? … ¿Y cuántos, más o menos, al día?
>
> **CA:** Una curiositat: els correus dels hostes, els contesteu vosaltres o els porta direcció? … I quants, més o menys, cada dia?

**What happens next — five cases**

| Reception says | You do |
|---|---|
| *«Ahora sale»* | Wait standing, envelope in hand. When they come out: §2.3 |
| *«No está»* / *«Está reunido»* | *«Sin problema. ¿Me dices cómo se llama, para escribirle directamente y no molestaros más? ¿Y a qué hora suele estar?»* Leave the envelope with the name written on it. Tracker: name, time, «envelope left». |
| *«Déjalo aquí, ya se lo paso»* | *«Perfecto, gracias. ¿Cómo se llama, para ponerle el nombre en el sobre?»* Write it on the envelope in front of them — it gets delivered. |
| *«¿De parte de qué empresa? ¿Es comercial?»* | Honest: *«Sí, es una herramienta que lleva la operativa del hotel: el resumen del día, los correos de huéspedes ya redactados, las llegadas, la ficha de cada huésped. Por eso quería que le llegara al director.»* |
| *«Aquí no atendemos a comerciales»* | *«Lo entiendo, perdona la interrupción. ¿Hay un correo de dirección donde pueda escribir?»* Leave the one-pager if they accept it. Thank them and go. **Never push at a desk** — the industry is small. |

Before leaving, if the receptionist was kind: *«Gracias, de verdad. Si esto sale, lo vais a notar vosotros antes que nadie.»*

### 2.3 The director comes out

Handshake, name, and set the frame so they relax: *«Dos minutos, de pie, y te dejo trabajar.»* Then run `pitch/GUION_60S.md` exactly — question first, phone at second 10, hand them the phone at screen 2, close with the question, silence.

**Four possible endings, and what to say:**

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| *«Interesante, mándamelo»* | *«Te lo mando esta tarde. ¿A qué correo?»* Then: *«Y para que no se quede en un correo más, ¿lo vemos veinte minutos con calma — el jueves a las 12 o el viernes a las 11? Vengo yo.»* |
| *«Enséñamelo ahora»* | The 10-minute version on your phone: brief (1 min) → inbox, before arrival and in-house (4) → arrivals and one guest's record (2) → the locked tiles, *what's coming* (1) → close (§9). Don't open the laptop in the lobby. |
| *«Ahora no es buen momento»* | *«Claro. Te dejo esto, que se lee en dos minutos. ¿Te va mejor la semana que viene, martes o jueves?»* Get the email either way. |
| An objection | §8. Answer once, briefly, then return to the two time slots. |

The close at the door is always **two concrete slots this week or next**, never *«cuando te vaya bien»*.

### 2.4 In the street, sixty seconds

Before the next hotel, update the tracker row: director's name, whether you met them, their email, PMS if you heard it, who owns the inbox, *first channel = walk-in*, next action with a date, and **their own words**: where the day goes, and what they never get round to. The first is your next email subject and, later, your homepage; the second is what you sell them from *what's coming* (§0.1).

### 2.5 The same-day email — before 21:00

See §4, templates **E1** (didn't meet the director) and **E2** (did).

## 3. Phone

### 3.1 How to call

Call the hotel's main number and ask for the director **by name**. Same windows as visits: 11:45–13:30 or 16:30–18:00. Before 10:00 directors are in the morning meeting.

What call-recording data says works on a first call ([Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/how-to-cold-call)):

- **Full name and company first.** No mystery.
- **Give the reason early.** *«Te llamo por…»* — «the reason for my call is» lifted success 2.1×.
- **Never ask «¿te pillo en mal momento?»** — that opener cut success by 40%.
- **Sell the meeting, not the product.** Discovery belongs in the demo. One value line, then the ask.
- **Ask for the calendar**: *«¿Tienes la agenda a mano?»* and lock a slot before hanging up.
- A warm *«¿Qué tal, cómo va la semana?»* works well when you've met or written before — Gong found a «how have you been» opener performed best of all. It sounds false on a truly cold call; skip it there.

Up to three attempts per director inside the sprint. An attempt where you don't speak to them isn't a touch.

### 3.2 Reception, on the phone

> Hola, buenos días. Soy Oriol Pellicer, de Fondas. ¿Me pasas con [Nombre], por favor?

If asked what it's about:

> Le escribí la semana pasada sobre la operativa del hotel. Es para quedar veinte minutos.

If they're not in:

> ¿A qué hora suele estar? … Perfecto, le llamo entonces. ¿Me dejas su correo, por si acaso?

### 3.3 The director — after a visit or an email (warm)

> Hola [Nombre], ¿qué tal, cómo va la semana? Soy Oriol, de Fondas. *(pausa)*
> Te llamo por el sobre que dejé el martes en recepción.
> En una frase: Fondas lleva la operativa del hotel todo el día — el resumen escrito antes de que abras, cada correo de huésped redactado con la voz del hotel, las llegadas, la ficha de cada huésped. Tú solo revisas y decides. Y cada semana hace más.
> Estoy eligiendo tres hoteles fundadores en Barcelona. ¿Tienes la agenda a mano? Veinte minutos en tu hotel — ¿jueves a las 12 o viernes a las 11?

### 3.4 The director — fully cold (Madrid, coastal)

> Hola [Nombre], soy Oriol Pellicer, de Fondas, desde Barcelona.
> Te llamo porque trabajo con hoteles independientes en una cosa muy concreta: todo el trabajo administrativo que se come el día de un hotel. Fondas se conecta a tu PMS y al correo del hotel y lo lleva — el resumen del día, cada correo de huésped redactado, las llegadas, la ficha de cada huésped — y cada semana hace más. Estoy eligiendo los primeros hoteles fundadores.
> No te lo voy a contar por teléfono. ¿Tienes la agenda a mano para veinte minutos por videollamada esta semana? — o, si estás en la costa, subo yo.

### 3.5 What they say on the phone

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| *«Estoy liado ahora»* | *«Por eso no te lo cuento ahora. Solo dime un hueco: ¿jueves o viernes?»* |
| *«Mándame información»* | *«Claro, te la mando ahora mismo. Para que no se pierda entre correos: dejamos veinte minutos el jueves, y si al leerla ves que no te encaja, me escribes y lo cancelo. ¿Te parece?»* |
| *«No nos interesa»* | *«Entendido. ¿Es por el momento, o porque el día a día ya lo tenéis resuelto?»* — listen, write it down, thank them. That answer is data. |
| *«Habla con [otra persona]»* | *«Perfecto. ¿Me dices su nombre y le digo que me lo has recomendado tú?»* |

**Leaving a message with reception** (hotels rarely have voicemail):

> ¿Le puedes decir que ha llamado Oriol, de Fondas, por lo de la operativa del hotel? Le vuelvo a llamar mañana a mediodía; si prefiere, mi móvil es [móvil].

## 6. The 20-minute demo

**Before it:** add their Google account as an OAuth test user the day it's booked (Gmail connect fails live otherwise); the demo hotel seeded with realistic Spanish guest names; phone and laptop both logged in; hotspot ready. **Where:** in their office, on your laptop, is best.

Walk them through **a day in their hotel, and a stay from booking to departure** — never "the morning". The brief is the first two minutes, not the product.

| Min | What you do |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | *«¿Dónde se os va el día —a ti y a recepción—? ¿Y qué es lo que nunca llegáis a hacer?»* Let them talk. Write down both answers: the first is the pain for today, the second is what layer 2 (§0.1) sells. |
| 3–5 | **Home and the brief.** *«Así empieza el día: a las 6:30 ya está escrito.»* Two lines aloud, then move on. |
| 5–11 | **Communications, both windows.** *Upcoming*: a pre-arrival email, the reservation beside it, the draft in the hotel's voice. *In-house*: a guest asking for something mid-stay. Edit one word live and send. *«Fondas lee tus datos y escribe esto. Tú decides qué sale — a cualquier hora, no solo por la mañana.»* |
| 11–13 | **Arrivals & departures and one guest's record.** The ETA chaser, then open a returning guest: tags, notes, *lo que pidió la última vez*. *«Esto es lo que sabe una buena recepcionista con diez años en la casa.»* |
| 13–14 | **Ask, and ⌘K.** One question in plain Spanish (*«¿quién llega tarde hoy y no ha dicho la hora?»*). |
| 14–16 | **What's coming.** Open Home › *Personalizar* and show the locked tiles. *«Esto es lo que viene: reseñas, tarifas en el resumen, extras antes de la llegada, el plan de estancia de cada huésped, y después pisos, F&B, personal. La ficha del huésped y las dos bandejas que acabas de ver no existían hace un mes. Los hoteles fundadores decidís el orden.»* Link it to their answer to *«¿qué nunca llegáis a hacer?»*. |
| 16–18 | **The labour close** (below), with *their* number. Then: *«¿Esto se parece a algo que os quitaría trabajo?»* and stop talking. |
| 18–20 | The close (§9). |

**Apaleo exception:** skip minutes 3–5 and open on Communications. Apaleo Copilot ships a brief; don't invite the comparison.

**The labour close.** Price against hours of a person's work — never against a person, and never against a ChatGPT subscription (reasoning and sources: `GTM_STRATEGY.md` §3.2.1). **Use their number, not ours**: ask how many hours a day go on guest email, arrivals, questions and admin across reception and management, then do the sum out loud. Say *«aunque Fondas solo os quitara la mitad»* — it's honest, and it's still a big number.

> ¿Cuántas horas al día se os van, entre todos, en correos de huéspedes, llegadas, preguntas y papeleo? … Vale, digamos dos. Son unas sesenta horas al mes. A coste de empresa de recepción, más de mil euros al mes. Aunque Fondas solo os quitara la mitad, son más de quinientos. Fondas cuesta 199 al mes, plano — 149 para siempre si entráis ahora como hotel fundador — y los primeros sesenta días, nada. Y lo que te he enseñado en *lo que viene* entra en ese precio.

A cheat sheet for the sum (30 days a month; employer cost per hour from `GTM_STRATEGY.md` §3.2.1):

| Hours a day, across the team | Hours a month | At receptionist cost (€18.50/h) | At head-of-reception cost (€30.80/h) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 30 | €555 | €924 |
| 2 | 60 | €1,110 | €1,848 |
| 3 | 90 | €1,665 | €2,772 |

## 10. Onboarding — the 30-minute setup

1. **Before the call:** their Google account added as an OAuth test user; PMS confirmed (MEWS, Apaleo, or the Sheet source with a sample export in hand).
2. Create their account → onboarding → connect the PMS → first sync.
3. **Generate a preview brief live, on the call.** This is the moment they get it. Don't skip it.
4. Connect Gmail.
5. Fill **Hotel profile & tone** together, conversationally — including the paid extras with prices. The drafts get good here.
6. Set brief recipients, send hour and **language** — Spanish or Catalan for a local director. An English brief kills it.
7. **Open a WhatsApp thread before hanging up.** It's how you'll hear that something broke.

**Week 1:** read their brief every morning before they do, and fix what's wrong before they report it. A broken pilot is worse than a late one.

## 11. During the pilot: day 30 and day 60

**Watch:** draft acceptance (sent as-is / minor edit / major edit / discarded — `lib/draft-acceptance.ts` measures it; the bar is >60%), brief opens before arrival, questions asked in Ask per week, and unprompted feature requests.

**Week 2, the 15-minute call — five questions:** (1) What's wrong or missing in the brief? (2) Which drafts did you edit, and why? (3) What do you no longer do that you used to? (4) What would make you stop using it? (5) What would you pay for it? Paste the answers verbatim into `PILOT_FEEDBACK.md`.

**Day 30, the commitment** — the real sales conversation. Ask while momentum is high, not at the end:

> Llevas un mes usándolo, y has visto salir [lo que se haya publicado ese mes]. ¿Seguís a partir del día 61? Si me dices que sí ahora, os quedáis el precio de fundador —149 al mes para siempre, con todo lo que venga— y hasta el día 61 no pagáis nada. Si la respuesta es no, dime qué falta; me sirve igual.

**Day 60, the decision.** A yes at day 30 gets confirmed; a *no todavía* at day 30 gets its second and last conversation, on what's on the screen that day.

Then two pricing questions, whatever they answer — four honest replies decide whether a higher tier is a 2027 conversation or a Q4 one:

> ¿A qué precio te parecería demasiado caro? ¿Y a qué precio tan barato que te haría dudar?

**And ask for the sentence:** *«Si tuvieras que contarle a otro director qué hace Fondas, ¿qué le dirías? ¿Me dejas usarlo con tu nombre?»* Get the yes in writing.

A *no* with a reason is a good pilot outcome. A drifting *maybe* is the only bad one — the two dates in the agreement prevent it.
