Route 2 is the definition, and Routes 1, 3, 4 and 5 are equally important entry points depending on who you're talking to. The sentence and the definition were settled the same day with Axelrod's structure (what it is, what the guest gets and what the team gets) and option D (§1.1). **Pending:** move it into `PILOT_PLAYBOOK.md` and `POSITIONING_V3.md`, which are still the ones that govern the pitch and the website (§8).

## 0. Who we're talking to and where we start

**Who buys and uses Fondas: the hotel team, not the guest.** The guest comes out ahead, but the person who lives the problem, decides and pays is someone at the hotel:

| Persona | What they are to Fondas | What they care about |
|---|---|---|
| **Receptionist** | Daily user | Not being swallowed up by the shift. Not looking bad in front of the guest |
| **Front office manager** | User and internal champion | Their team, the inbox, arrivals, complaints |
| **Operations or hotel director** | The decision-maker in a single hotel | Knowing what's happening today, nothing slipping through, the team, the margin |
| **Owner or general manager** | The one who pays | Margin, risk, standing out, what the reviews say |
| **Group or chain** (corporate operations, DPO, IT) | The decision-maker and the one who can veto | The same standard across all hotels, control and compliance |

**The proposition speaks to hotels, with no qualifier.** It doesn't say *independent*, *boutique* or *small*: shutting the door on a group in the first sentence is shutting the funnel. **Where we start selling** is a different matter: independent hotels of 20 to 80 rooms, because they decide quickly and without an IT or procurement department (`GTM_STRATEGY.md` §4.3). It's the same rule set in `POSITIONING_V3.md` §1.4: *narrow on who we write to, broad on who the page speaks to*.

## 1. What Fondas is (Route 2, the definition)

**When anyone asks *"what is Fondas?"*, whoever they are, this is the answer.** Then you move on to the route for their persona (§2). The four pieces say the same thing in three parts:

1. **What it is:** the AI that works with everything the hotel already uses.
2. **What the guest gets:** a more personal stay.
3. **What the team gets:** more time to give it.

The team is the protagonist; the guest is the reason.

**The sentence** (bio, signature, first line of an email or a walk-in, end of a post):

> **Fondas is the AI that works with everything your hotel already uses, so every guest gets a more personal stay, and your team has more time to give it.**
>
> ES: *Fondas es la IA que trabaja con todo lo que ya usa tu hotel, para que cada huésped tenga una estancia más personal y tu equipo, más tiempo para dársela.*

**The headline** (website, one-pager, first slide):

> **Your whole hotel in one place. AI-native.**
>
> ES: *Todo tu hotel en un solo sitio. Nativo en IA.*

**The tagline** (end of a post, a talk, the short signature):

> **More time with guests. Less behind screens.**
>
> ES: *Más tiempo con los huéspedes. Menos delante de la pantalla.*

**The definition, in 30 seconds** (in person, by phone, at the start of the demo):

> Fondas is the AI that works with everything your hotel already uses: the PMS, email and the rest. Every day it leaves the team's work ready (the daily brief, arrivals and guest replies, each one knowing who is writing and what they've booked), and it answers any question about the hotel on the spot: *"who's returning this week?"*, *"how's Saturday looking?"*. The team just reviews and decides. The result: more personal stays and a team with more time for guests.
>
> ES: *Fondas es la IA que trabaja con todo lo que ya usa tu hotel: el PMS, el correo y el resto. Cada día le deja al equipo el trabajo preparado (el resumen del día, las llegadas y las respuestas a los huéspedes, cada una sabiendo quién escribe y qué ha reservado), y responde al momento a cualquier pregunta sobre el hotel: «¿quién repite esta semana?», «¿cómo va el sábado?». El equipo solo revisa y decide. El resultado: estancias más personales y un equipo con más tiempo para los huéspedes.*

**The paragraph** (website, one-pager, email):

> For the teams who run a hotel, from front desk to management, Fondas is the AI where the day's operations are run. It works with everything the hotel already uses (the PMS, email and the rest) and prepares the work: the daily brief, arrivals and guest communications. And at any time you can ask it anything about the hotel, in plain language, and it answers with the real data. Every reply to a guest knows who is writing and what they've booked, and every returning guest arrives with their record, so every stay is more personal. The team spends less time behind screens and more with guests. It's AI-native: a PMS copilot is AI added to a legacy program and only sees its own part; Fondas has been built with AI from day one and sees the whole hotel. And unlike a ChatGPT open at the front desk, it knows your hotel, writes in its tone and sends nothing without approval.

**Why this is the main one.** It's the only one of the five routes that describes *what Fondas is*, and not just *what you get*. It works just as well for 30 rooms as for a group. It's the first screen of the demo. And it marks the difference from what the hotel uses today: the PMS copilot only sees the PMS, ChatGPT sees nothing of the hotel, and the notebook and the Excel sheet prepare nothing.

**What the definition says and what shows it in the demo:**

| Piece | What it says | In the demo |
|---|---|---|
| **Works with everything you already use** | PMS, email and the rest, in one place | Home and the connections in Settings |
| **Leaves the work ready** | Brief done, drafts, arrival times requested; the person decides | The daily brief, Arrivals, Communications (before arrival and during the stay), the approval |
| **Answers any question** | About the hotel, in plain language, with the real data | Ask: *"who's returning this week?"*, *"how's Saturday looking?"*, *"who's arriving late today?"*. Let the director ask their own question |
| **More personal stays** | Replies that know who is writing; returning guests with their record | The matched reservation next to the draft, the guest record. **Later:** the stay plan |
| **More time to give it** | The time saved goes back to the guest | The hours a week that no longer go on email (Route 1) and the director's own words when they tell you |

**The chat is the "wow" moment of the demo.** The brief and the drafts are understood; the free question is *felt*. Hand the director the phone and let them ask something about their own hotel. If the answer lands, the demo is done.

**The morning is how the demo starts, not what we sell.** What we sell is the whole day; if it sounds like *"the morning brief"*, a director replies *"I can do that myself in a few minutes"* (`POSITIONING_V3.md` §10).

**Prepared answers:**
- *"Does it work with [Outlook, Opera, Cloudbeds…]?"* → *"We connect it during onboarding."*
- *"Does this change my PMS?"* → *"No, it works with it and brings together what's scattered today."*
- *"How much does it cost?"* → *"The first sixty days, nothing. After that it depends on the hotel, above all its size, and we talk about it at the end of the pilot, once you know it's useful."* **Never give a figure:** price is not mentioned in any document or sales conversation (decided 15 Oct).
- *"What if the AI gets it wrong?"* → *"Nothing goes out without your approval, and it's all logged."* (Route 4)
- *"What does AI-native mean?"* → *"It's been built with AI from day one; it isn't AI added on top of a legacy program."*
- *"How is this different from ChatGPT?"* → *"ChatGPT knows nothing about your hotel. Fondas sees your bookings and your email, and answers with your data."*

**What we are not** (so we don't lose the sale over one word):
- **We don't replace anyone.** We share out the work: *"The AI that runs the operations, and you, the guests"*. Enginy started out selling *"AI does it all"* and had to move to copilot because the market wasn't buying it. Axelrod sells *"hotels that run themselves"*; we sell a team with more time for the guest.
- **We're not a PMS.** We work with it.
- **We're not a guest chatbot.** The chat is for the team; today everything that reaches a guest goes through a person at the hotel.

### 1.1 Where the sentence comes from, and the alternatives

**Where it comes from.** The structure comes from **Axelrod**, a direct competitor (Y Combinator, Summer 2026, AI agents for boutique and luxury hotels, tagline *"Hotels that run themselves"*). Their description: *"Axelrod orchestrates work across your existing systems to deliver hyperpersonalized stays, giving your team more time with guests and less time behind screens."* It works because it says three things in one sentence: what it does, what the guest gets and what the team gets. **We keep the structure, not the words**: in an industry this small, using their sentence would make us look like a copy.

**Why ours is better for us (decided 15 Oct, option D):**
- **"The AI that works with everything your hotel already uses"** says what it is and that nothing has to change, without jargon ("orchestrate", "systems").
- **"A more personal stay"** rather than "hyperpersonalized": it's what the product does today (the guest record, replies that know who is writing). What would make it *hyper*, the stay plan, is "Coming soon".
- **"And your team, more time to give it"** joins the two gains: the stay is more personal *because* the team has time. It's the opposite of Axelrod's promise (the hotel runs itself), and it answers the fear of *"does this replace my receptionist?"*.

**AI-native (decided 15 Oct).** It goes in the headline and as a prepared answer. The sentence already starts with *"the AI that…"*, so there's no need to repeat it. It's what separates us from the PMS copilot, and it's Kai's thesis: the big players add layers of AI on top of what they already had, and the new ones rebuild the work from scratch. **Never** use *LLM*, *agent*, *model* or the AI provider's name in the first sentence; if they ask, the answer is on `/trust`.

**Translations of the sentence:**
- **ES:** *Fondas es la IA que trabaja con todo lo que ya usa tu hotel, para que cada huésped tenga una estancia más personal y tu equipo, más tiempo para dársela.*
- **CA:** *Fondas és la IA que treballa amb tot el que ja fa servir el teu hotel, perquè cada hoste tingui una estada més personal i el teu equip, més temps per donar-li.*

| Discarded alternative | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **Fondas coordinates your hotel's work on top of the tools you already use, so every stay is more personal and your team spends more time with guests and less behind screens.** (A) | Closest to Axelrod | Long; "coordinates" is cold |
| **Fondas connects to everything your hotel already uses and leaves the day's work done, so…** (B) | Product-centred | Longer and less memorable |
| **Fondas brings your whole hotel onto one screen and prepares the day's work, so…** (C) | Close to what we had | Doesn't say it's AI |
| **Your PMS, your email and everything else, working together: Fondas prepares the day so your team is with guests, not with the screen.** (E) | Clear contrast | Two sentences in one; doesn't say what it is |
| **Fondas, AI-native, runs the day's work across the systems your hotel already uses…** (the first attempt) | Follows the structure | Sounds translated in Spanish; "system… systems" |
| Axelrod's sentence, translated | It's very good | It's theirs. Not used |

**How to test it:** as the first sentence in the next walk-ins, right after introducing yourself. If the person nods or asks *"how?"*, it works. If there's no time at the door, say the headline and the tagline.

## 2. The four entry points

All four are equally important. **What changes is who's in front of you.** Each one opens with a different problem (§3) and always comes back to the definition in §1.

### Route 1 — Time: "Less paperwork, more hotel"

> Fondas takes care of the repetitive front office work (email, arrivals, the daily brief) so your team can spend its time running the hotel.

- **For whom:** front office manager and receptionists. It's almost always the first person you meet on a walk-in, and the one who takes you to the director.
- **Opens with:** P1 (hours) and P2 (the inbox).
- **What proves it:** a draft reply to one of their real emails, done in seconds. Do the maths out loud with them: emails per day × minutes per email. Later on, the draft acceptance figure (`lib/draft-acceptance.ts`).
- **Bridge to Route 2:** *"And since everything's in one place, you don't waste time looking for things either."*
- **Careful:** talk about hours given back, never about jobs saved. If you don't put it in numbers ("about X hours a week"), it sounds like what every piece of software promises.

### Route 3 — Team: "Every shift working like your best shift"

> The hotel's tone, what's known about each guest and how things are done don't leave when someone leaves. A new joiner replies like someone who's been there for years.

- **For whom:** hotel director, owner and, above all, groups (*the same standard across all hotels*).
- **Opens with:** P4 (knowledge leaves) and the talent shortage in P1.
- **What proves it:** a draft in the house tone, the profile of the returning guest and Ask used the way a new joiner would use it (*"what do we offer someone arriving late?"*).
- **Bridge to Route 2:** *"Because everything the hotel knows is in one place, and not in the heads of two people."*
- **Careful:** the benefit shows in the medium term, not in 20 minutes. You'll need a pilot with staff turnover to have the case. Don't say *"your best receptionist, on every shift"*: it sounds like replacing them.

### Route 4 — AI under control: "Your team already uses AI. Make it the hotel's"

> Fondas brings AI inside the hotel, with rules: nothing goes out without approval, everything is logged and the team is trained, as European law requires.

- **For whom:** owner, general manager and, in a group, DPO and IT. In any conversation, it's also **the closing argument**.
- **Opens with:** P5 (shadow AI and the law).
- **What proves it:** Oversight › AI (the log of what the AI does), Settings › AI (the five-minute training with a record per hotel), the `/trust` page and the approval button.
- **Bridge to Route 2:** *"And it's the same tool you run the day with. It isn't a separate compliance layer."*
- **Careful:** to a small hotel it sells fear, and they're in no hurry. It needs educational content first (G1, G2). Never say *"fine"*, *"certificate"* or *"the law prohibits"* (§3, P5).

### Route 5 — Category: "The hotel's operating system"

> The hotel's operating system, AI-native. A single place where the team sees the day, replies to guests and asks whatever it needs to know about the hotel, and where the rest of the operations will gradually come in.

- **For whom:** the website, investors, press, the LinkedIn bio and the leadership of a group that thinks in systems.
- **Opens with:** the ambition, not a problem. It's the label that sits on top of Route 2.
- **What proves it:** the trajectory. What has shipped in recent weeks and the locked pieces in Home › Customise (`POSITIONING_V3.md` §10). Always in the future tense: revenue, housekeeping and the stay plan are *on the way*.
- **Bridge to Route 2:** Route 2 is what the operating system does today.
- **Careful:** to a receptionist *"operating system"* means nothing, and it gets confused with the PMS. The answer to *"does this change my PMS?"* is in §1. It never goes on its own.

### Who, which route and in what order

| In front of you is… | Open with | Continue with | Close with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Receptionist or front office manager | **1** Time | **2** Definition | **4** *"Nothing goes out without your approval"* |
| Operations or hotel director | **2** Definition | **1** Time | **3** Team |
| Owner or general manager | **2** Definition | **3** Team + margin (P1) | **4** AI under control |
| Group or chain (operations) | **5** Category | **2** Definition | **3** Standard across hotels, then **4** |
| DPO or IT | **4** AI under control | **2** Definition | `/trust` and the compliance pack |
| Investor, website, LinkedIn | **5** Category | **2** Definition | **4** as a differentiator |

**Two rules:**
1. *"What is Fondas?"* is always answered with §1, whoever it is.
2. Every conversation ends on Route 4. Not as a fear argument, but as the answer to the question everyone asks without saying it: *"what if the AI says something stupid to a guest?"*

## 3. The problems, seen from the team's side

**The rule:** you open the conversation with what the team **already feels**. What's **latent** becomes educational content, so that by the time you arrive they've already heard it. It's Kai's *allbound*.

| | Problem | Do they feel it already? | Evidence | Route | Fondas today | Fondas tomorrow |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** | The team can't take any more, and there's no one to hire | **Yes** | High | 1, 3 | The whole product | — |
| **P2** | The inbox: the same thing twenty times, in five languages, and late | **Yes** | Medium (no Spanish data) | 1 | Communications | Automatic sending with an AI disclosure line (B19) |
| **P3** | The day starts blind: everything is scattered | **Yes** | Our own and the pilots' experience | **2** | Brief, Arrivals, Home, Ask | Brief via WhatsApp |
| **P4** | Knowledge leaves with every person who leaves | Medium | Medium | 3 | Guest profile, learned tone, Ask | The hotel's own recommendations |
| **P5** | The team already uses AI, on their own and without control | **No** (latent) | High (law), medium (US data) | 4 | Approval, AI log, training, `/trust` | — |
| **P6** | The guest wants more personalisation, and the same team has to deliver it | Medium | Medium (US surveys) | 3, 5 | Guest profile | Stay plan, "Around the hotel" |

### P1 — The team can't take any more, and there's no one to hire

**What's happening.** Spanish hotels are billing more than ever: a RevPAR of €129.9 from January to September 2025, 6.3% more than in 2024. But costs, staff included, are growing faster than revenue, and the margin isn't rising. An urban hotel runs at a GOP of 32 to 34% and depends on keeping labour costs under control ([Hosteltur, Dec 2025](https://www.hosteltur.com/173142_muchos-hoteles-facturan-mas-que-nunca-pero-con-margenes-mas-reducidos.html), with HotStats data). And hiring is hard: 75% of hospitality businesses acknowledge difficulties filling positions, although most talk about "some difficulties" and only a minority about a critical situation (ManpowerGroup, *Talent Shortage 2026*, via [El Español, May 2026](https://vandal.elespanol.com/random/es-oficial-la-hosteleria-en-espana-esta-en-crisis-y-el-75-de-los-negocios-sufre-para-contratar-personal/42001.html)).

**How each person says it:**
- Front office manager: *"My best receptionist spends the morning answering emails about parking."*
- Director: *"I can't hire another person, and I can't find one either."*
- Owner: *"We're billing more and earning the same."*

**What Fondas does today.** It prepares the repetitive work (the brief, the drafts, the arrival times) so that a person only has to review and decide.

### P2 — The inbox: the same thing twenty times, in five languages, and late

**What's happening.** A large part of a hotel's email is repeated questions (parking, early check-in, pets, how to get there), in several languages. Every slow reply is a direct booking that goes to an OTA or a first impression that gets off to a bad start. There's no Spanish figure: a study that wrote 958 emails to hotels in Poland measured an average first response time of **16 h 56 min**, with a median of **12 h** ([Cognivis](https://cognivis.ai/hotels/landings/response-time-study-old/)). It's another country and it's published by a vendor: use it as a reference, not as a headline. G4 proposes getting our own figure.

**How the team says it:** *"Every morning I have 30 emails and half of them are the same question."*

**What Fondas does today.** Communications: drafts in the hotel's tone and with the booking details, and nothing goes out without approval. **The metric that proves it** is draft acceptance (>60%).

### P3 — The day starts blind: everything is scattered

**This is the Route 2 problem.**

**What's happening.** Information is scattered across the PMS, email, the OTA extranet, the night shift's WhatsApp and paper notes. Every morning and at every shift change, someone pieces together what's happening today: who's arriving, at what time, who's returning, what's missing, what was left pending. In a hotel without a 24-hour front desk, not knowing what time someone is arriving is an operational problem, not a matter of convenience. In a group, multiply it by every hotel and every PMS.

**How each person says it:**
- Director: *"I don't know what kind of day I've got until ten."*
- Front desk: *"I find out they're arriving at 23:00 when they ring the bell."*
- Group: *"To find out how each hotel is doing I have to call each director."*

**What Fondas does today.** The daily brief, Arrivals and check-ins (it asks for the arrival time), Home and Ask.

### P4 — Knowledge leaves with every person who leaves

**What's happening.** The hotel's tone, what the returning guest likes, how a typical complaint gets resolved… all of that lives in the heads of two or three people. Turnover in hospitality is very high: the same Hosteltur article cites hotels that have brought it down **from 70–80% to 40–50%**, which added 3–4 points of GOP. Every time someone leaves, training the new person comes out of the front office manager's time. In a group, on top of that, each hotel ends up with its own standard.

**How the team says it:** *"Every season I explain the same things all over again."*

**What Fondas does today.** The guest profile (history and preferences), drafts that learn the house tone, and Ask. **Tomorrow:** the hotel's own recommendations (`GUEST_EXPERIENCE_SPEC.md` §6.1) and soft-skills training (`ROADMAP.md` §3.6).

### P5 — The team already uses AI, on their own and without control

**This is the Route 4 problem.**

**What's happening (team).** People already use AI at work, on their own. In a TELUS Digital survey of 1,000 employees at large US companies (January 2025), **68%** use public assistants with their personal account, **57%** admit to having entered sensitive information, **31%** personal data (names, emails, phone numbers) and **21%** customer information ([TELUS Digital](https://www.telusdigital.com/about/newsroom/telus-digital-survey-reveals-enterprise-employees-use-of-shadow-ai)). It isn't hotel data or Spanish data: present it as such.

**What's happening (company and law), precisely:**
- **AI Act, art. 4 (AI literacy).** It has applied since 2 February 2025 to anyone who **uses** AI in their professional activity, not only to those who build it. The Digital Omnibus (Reg. (EU) 2026/1744, published in July 2026) **softened** it: you no longer have to *ensure* a level of training, but *take measures to support it*. It's still mandatory, from 2 August 2026 national authorities can supervise it, and it's wise to keep evidence of what you've done ([FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/the-ai-act-implementation-timeline-what-changes-under-the-ai-omnibus/), [Dastra](https://www.dastra.eu/en/blog/ai-literacy-why-the-omnibus-changed-the-rules-of-the-game-without-changing-the/60562)).
- **AI Act, art. 50 (transparency).** From August 2026 you have to tell a person when they're talking to an AI (for example, a guest chatbot). Marking of generated text applies to systems like Fondas from 2 December 2026 (`AI_ACT_PROMPTS.md`).
- **Spain.** On 26 May 2026 the Council of Ministers approved the draft law for the good use and governance of AI (*proyecto de ley para el buen uso y la gobernanza de la IA*), which is being sent to the Cortes. It's going through parliament and not yet in force. Supervision will fall to AESIA. The big fines are for serious infringements and prohibited or high-risk systems, not for a hotel that drafts emails ([RTVC](https://rtvc.es/consejo-ministros-ley-limitar-uso-inteligencia-artificial-26-mayo-2026/), [Xataka](https://www.xataka.com/legislacion-y-derechos/espana-aprueba-su-ley-ia-deepfakes-sexuales-fuera-etiqueta-ia-obligatoria-multas-millonarias-al-sector-privado)). **Don't use the €35M as a threat.**
- **GDPR.** Pasting a guest's email into a tool the hotel has no data processing agreement with is, at the very least, a GDPR problem, and it's the most concrete risk today. *Have the lawyer validate this (`AI_ACT_PROMPTS.md` §L) before using it in public.*

**How each person says it** when the penny drops:
- Director: *"I have no idea what they're putting into ChatGPT at the front desk."*
- DPO: *"Where's the log?"*

**What Fondas does today.** The same work, but inside a tool with a contract, without the provider training on the data, with human approval, the log of what the AI does (Oversight › AI), the five-minute training with a record per hotel (Settings › AI) and `/trust` for the DPO. **No "ChatGPT at the front desk" has this.** The line you can use is in `AI_ACT_PROMPTS.md` §P.

### P6 — The guest wants more personalisation, and the same team has to deliver it

**What's happening.** What sets a hotel apart is no longer its facilities but how it treats people. In a TakeUp study of 350 independent-hotel travellers (2025), **95%** say personalised recommendations would make them more likely to book, and **45%** would pay more for unique experiences. Only **11%** look for the lowest rate ([Hotel Business](https://hotelbusiness.com/report-independent-hotel-guests-want-personalization-over-price/)). In another, commissioned by Mews from 2,000 US travellers, **68%** would be loyal to a hotel for a personalised experience rather than for points ([TravelDailyNews](https://www.traveldailynews.com/statistics-trends/personalization-beats-points-68-of-travelers-prefer-tailored-experiences-over-hotel-loyalty-rewards/)). Both come from industry vendors and use samples from outside Spain: use them as a trend.

**Why it's a team problem, not a guest problem:** the guest sets the bar, but the same team has to meet it, with no extra people, and they're judged by the reviews.

**How each person says it:**
- Director: *"I want them to know the child's name, but I don't even have time for the email."*
- Owner: *"I can't compete on pools with the chain next door."*

**What Fondas does today.** The guest profile and in-stay communications. **Tomorrow:** the stay plan (`GUEST_EXPERIENCE_SPEC.md`). Under the rule in `POSITIONING_V3.md` §0.4, that's talked about **in the future tense**.

### Set aside for now

- **Reports for the owner.** It's a real problem, but the owner's brief is in "Later" (`ROADMAP.md` §3.6). It will come back with the first group.
- **Revenue, upselling and OTA dependence.** They're big problems, but today we have the average rate in the brief, not a pricing engine. They go in the future tense (`POSITIONING_V3.md` §0.1).

| Yes | No |

| Your whole hotel in one place | A new PMS, or *"change your PMS"* |

## 6. Five educational hooks

The format is Kai's: **data point or law → why it affects you → what to do → (at the end, and in one line) Fondas.** The content has to be useful even if they never buy. Each hook feeds a route.

**G1 — The AI law already affects you, even if you don't know it** (Route 4)
- *Hook:* "If someone at your hotel uses AI to answer emails, since 2025 European law has asked that your team be trained. And since August 2026 there are authorities that can check it."
- *Content:* what art. 4 asks for after the Omnibus (reasonable measures, not a qualification), what counts as evidence and three things to do this week.
- *Bridge:* Fondas comes with the five-minute training and keeps the record.
- *Watch out:* don't say *"fine"*. Say *"being able to prove it"*.

**G2 — What is your front desk pasting into ChatGPT?** (Route 4; it's Denis's angle)
- *Hook:* "In a survey of 1,000 employees, 57% admitted to entering sensitive information into public AI assistants, and 68% use them with their personal account. Do you know what your front desk does with guests' emails?"
- *Content:* why it's a GDPR problem, three simple rules for the team and how to ask about it without it sounding like an accusation.
- *Bridge:* the same work, inside a tool with a contract and a log.

**G3 — You're billing more than ever and earning the same** (Routes 1 and 3; it's the natural in-person opener)
- *Hook:* "RevPAR rose 6.3% in 2025, but an urban hotel's margin is still between 32 and 34%. Costs, and above all staff, are growing faster than revenue."
- *Content:* where front desk hours go (emails per day × minutes per email) and which tasks don't need your best person.
- *Bridge:* the hours Fondas gives back.

**G4 — How long does your hotel take to answer an email? (with our own data)** (Routes 1 and 2)
- *Idea:* do your own study. Write to 50 Barcelona hotels with an ordinary guest question (parking, late arrival) and measure how long they take and how many don't reply. **It's original content**, which is what works best on LinkedIn, and it also **leaves you with a prospect list sorted by pain**: the ones that take 30 hours are your ICP.
- *Hook:* "We wrote to 50 Barcelona hotels. Half took more than X hours."
- *Bridge:* all email in one place, with drafts ready in minutes and in the hotel's tone.
- *Watch out:* anonymise. Never publish a hotel's name: it's a small sector and tomorrow it could be your customer.

**G5 — The rooftop pool no longer impresses; being called by your name does** (Routes 3 and 5; the best content for owners)
- *Hook:* "95% of independent-hotel travellers would book more with recommendations made for them. Only 11% look for the lowest price."
- *Content:* three personalisation touches that don't require building work (the name, the occasion, a neighbourhood recommendation) and how a team with no time pulls them off.
- *Bridge:* the guest profile today; the stay plan, on the way (in the future tense).

**Pace.** Two posts a week (`GTM_STRATEGY.md` §4.14), alternating one the team already feels (G3, G4) with a latent one (G1, G2, G5). Ask your network to engage in the first 20 minutes and connect every week with the directors on the list.

## 7. How to validate it (in the next 10 conversations)

1. **Note the persona and the route.** In the tracker, for each conversation: the role of the person in front of you, the route you opened with and whether there was a demo. In ten conversations you'll see which route opens doors with which persona.
2. **Ask them to rank the problems.** Show P1–P6 on a card: *"Which of these takes up most of your time?"*. Note the order by role. If a front office manager and a director rank them differently, the table in §2 gets adjusted.
3. **Test the definition.** After saying §1, ask: *"How would you explain it to your boss?"*. If they repeat it back well, the definition works. If they say *"a PMS"* or *"a chatbot"*, it doesn't.
4. **Keep their exact words.** What they say in their own words is your next headline.
5. **Whatever wins goes into the playbook** (§8).
