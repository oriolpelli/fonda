On the medium — please read

These SVG studies are colour & composition roughs — they approximate the wash-and-wobble look, but a browser can't paint like a brush. Their job is to lock which scene, which palette, which crop. The final hero should be hand-painted by a watercolor illustrator (or generated with an image model) at 2× hero width — I've written a ready-to-use brief/prompt under every concept so you can hand it straight to an illustrator or paste it into an image tool.

How this lives with Signal

Your Pillows reference is the model: one painted hero + a set of small watercolor spot-vignettes, all floating on a clean near-white ground, while the rest of the UI stays flat and neutral. The illustration becomes the brand's single warm layer over the cool Signal system — warmth from the art, calm from the type. That's a coherent evolution, not a fight.

A matching set of small, loose watercolor spot illustrations on white, painted in the same warm Mediterranean palette and brush style as the hero: a brass room key, an espresso cup, an olive branch, a sun-lounger with a striped parasol, a small sailboat, a potted lemon tree, a breakfast tray. My recommendation

For the hero, lead with 01 La Casa (the villa & pines) — it's the closest to your Pillows reference, instantly says "beautiful independent hotel," and its symmetry leaves clean space for the headline. Keep 02 La Cala as the expressive alternate if you want the page to feel more like an art print than a product site.

But the real win is treating it as a system, exactly like Pillows: one hero painting + the spot-vignette set, all on white, with the flat Signal UI underneath. Commission the hero and ~6 vignettes from one watercolor illustrator in a single brief so the family is consistent — I've written those briefs above, ready to send or paste into an image model.

Two honest caveats: these SVGs are direction roughs, not the final look — real brush art will be far richer. And full-colour painting is a deliberate step away from Signal's "no illustration" rule, so it's a brand decision worth making on purpose (I'd frame it internally as "Signal + a warm illustrated layer").
