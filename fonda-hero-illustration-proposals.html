Fonda · Hero art direction · 5 proposals

A drawn welcome.

Five directions for a hand-drawn hotel scene behind the opening headline — garden and seaside, never woods. Each is shown in context with a sample headline, plus mood, palette, placement and how far it bends the Signal system.

Single-ink line art Garden / seaside Headline stays legible Illustrator-ready directions
One thing to decide first

Your current v2 "Signal" identity deliberately retired illustration — "type and space carry the brand," and "decorative illustration" is on the avoid list. A drawn hero is therefore a real, intentional evolution of the brand, closest in spirit to the old v1 "Warm" tone. The way to do it without breaking Signal is the constraint every proposal below follows: one restrained line drawing, one ink colour (navy or near-black), enormous negative space, and the illustration always subordinate to the headline — never a busy full-bleed scene. Think Vizcom's sketch-hero or Browserbase's single landscape at the base of the fold, not a decorated postcard.

01

Costa Serena — the sea horizon

The calmest option. A single horizon line runs the full width and doubles as the rule that separates the hero from the page. A low villa, one olive tree, a few wave strokes. The headline floats in open sky.

Direction

MoodCalm, boutique, coastal. Reads as "arrival."
PaletteNavy ink line on warm paper.
PlacementIllustration in the lower two-thirds; headline top-left in the sky.
MotionWaves drift a few px on load; sun fades in. Nothing loops.

Why it works · Signal fit

  • The horizon becomes the section divider — the illustration earns its keep structurally.
  • Maximum negative space; headline never fights the art.
  • Easiest to keep on-brand and to animate responsibly.
Signal fit · high
Effort · low
02

El Jardí — the courtyard

The warmest, most "hospitality" option. A small Mediterranean courtyard: an arched doorway, potted plants, a bistro table for two, a climbing vine. It says independent, characterful, human — the opposite of enterprise software.

Direction

MoodIntimate, boutique, hospitable. Most "small independent hotel."
PaletteNavy + a touch of ink for furniture. Warm paper.
PlacementScene sits on a ground-line at the base; headline upper-left.
MotionVine "grows" in on load (stroke-dash). Everything else still.

Why it works · Signal fit

  • Strongest emotional differentiation from Mews-style enterprise hero.
  • The table-for-two subtly signals the guest relationship Fonda protects.
  • Risk: most detail of the five — must be drawn with a light hand to stay Signal.
Signal fit · medium
Effort · medium
03

La Terrassa — the guest's view

Point-of-view from the property looking out: a terrace railing, two loungers, a parasol, the bay beyond with one sailboat. It sells the outcome — the calm the GM gets when the admin runs itself.

Direction

MoodLeisure, relief, "the view from calm."
PaletteNavy structure, ink furniture, warm paper.
PlacementForeground railing anchors the fold bottom; sky holds the headline.
MotionVery subtle parallax between railing and sea on scroll (respect reduced-motion).

Why it works · Signal fit

  • Sells the benefit (calm), not the building — pairs perfectly with an outcome headline.
  • Depth from just two planes; still very sparse.
  • Parallax is the one place to be careful — Signal says "no parallax," so keep it near-zero or drop it.
Signal fit · high
Effort · medium
04

La Façana — the facade mark

A frontal, near-symmetrical elevation of a whitewashed boutique hotel: shutters, flower boxes, a lantern, a small hanging "Fonda" sign, two cypress. The most ownable option — it can graduate into an icon / favicon / email header, not just a hero.

Direction

MoodCrafted, iconic, characterful. A little bit "inn sign."
PaletteNavy facade, ink cypress, navy flower dots.
PlacementSide-by-side: headline left, facade right (flips on mobile).
MotionDraw-on stroke, then the lantern glows once. Static after.

Why it works · Signal fit

  • Most reusable: the facade can become a brand mark, favicon, email header, loading state.
  • "Fonda" = a small Spanish inn — the facade literally is the brand story.
  • Side-by-side layout keeps type dominant, so it stays Signal-legal.
Signal fit · high
Effort · medium-high
05

Vista — the property, from above

An axonometric line drawing of the whole property: building, garden, a small pool, loungers, a path. The most modern and "product-y" of the five, and it rhymes with the "whole hotel on one screen" showcase further down the page.

Direction

MoodModern, systematic, "control room" — but warm.
PaletteNavy plot, ink building, dashed navy path.
PlacementHeadline left; the plot floats right, slightly below centre.
MotionElements settle in with a soft rise; the path draws last.

Why it works · Signal fit

  • Best conceptual match to the product ("the whole hotel, one screen").
  • Feels contemporary — closest to Signal's neutral, systematic voice.
  • Could scale into an animated "map" that lights up as surfaces connect during onboarding.
Signal fit · high
Effort · medium-high

My recommendation

If the goal is emotional warmth without breaking Signal, lead with 01 Costa Serena or 03 La Terrassa — the horizon does structural work, the risk is lowest, and an outcome-driven headline ("The admin runs. You host.") sits cleanly on top. They're also the easiest to ship and animate within your "minimal, purposeful" motion rule.

If you want a brand asset that pays off everywhere, commission 04 La Façana — the inn facade can become the mark, the favicon, the email header and the onboarding art, so one illustration investment carries the whole system. "Fonda" literally means a small inn, so the facade is the story.

Whichever you pick: keep it one drawing, one ink, mostly empty, and always behind or beside the headline — never a full-bleed scene.
