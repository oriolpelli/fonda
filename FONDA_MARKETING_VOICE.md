## 1. The repositioning (front office → hotel manager)

The old category word — "front office" — undersells the product. It sounds like an
inbox tool. But Fonda now spans three managerial jobs a hotel would otherwise hire
or juggle for:

- **Revenue** — watches competitor pricing and moves your rates; pushes upgrades
  and extras to fill rooms at better rates.
- **Guest experience** — pre-arrival messages and activity offers tailored to the
  guest (couple, family, business), not one-size-fits-all.
- **Operations** — drafts guest email in your voice, chases ETAs, and writes the
  morning brief.

So the through-line becomes **"the AI manager that runs your hotel's operations and
revenue, so you can run the hotel."** The human still owns hospitality and the
final call; Fonda owns the tireless, always-on legwork.

**One honesty guardrail stays.** Even at this bigger scope, keep the human in the
loop where it matters: guest emails are *drafted for your review*; rate moves and
offers are *within the rules you set*. Confident about the breadth, honest about
the control — that's still the voice (§2).

## 2. The voice, distilled from Sana

**Sana's moves:** declarative and calm; confident, not loud; concrete nouns and
verbs (name the real work); the human stays in the frame; editorial spacing, one
idea per line.

**Fonda's voice = Sana's calm, applied to running a hotel.** Warm, plain-spoken,
boutique not corporate. It should sound like a sharp operator who worked the night
shift for you — pricing, offers, inbox all handled — not like software bragging.

**Do**
- Lead with outcomes an owner feels: fuller rooms, better rates, a calm morning.
- Name the real jobs: pricing, upgrades, arrivals, the brief.
- Keep the human in control on the judgment calls — "drafted for you", "within
  your rules", "your call".
- Let a headline be a plain statement. Tension is fine; cleverness is a tax.

**Don't**
- Don't claim total autonomy: no "runs itself", "on autopilot", "set and forget".
  (Fonda does a lot on its own, but you still set the rules and approve the sends.)
- Don't build a headline on a pun or a mirror-image antithesis that breaks in
  translation.
- No hype adjectives, no exclamation marks, no emoji in marketing copy.

**Localization (ES/CA):** keep the informal **tú/tu**; translate the *idea*, not the
words; prefer everyday hotel terms (*tarifas*, *ocupación*, *huéspedes/hostes*,
*mejoras/millores* for upgrades, *extras*); read it aloud — if a native operator
wouldn't say it, rephrase.

### Subhead (rewritten for the bigger scope)

The old subhead only named email/arrivals/brief. This one covers revenue + guests +
ops. Works under all six options.

  competition, drives upgrades and extras, sends guests offers tailored to their

- **ES:** Un director con IA para tu hotel: ajusta tus tarifas según la
  competencia, impulsa mejoras y extras, envía a cada huésped ofertas a su medida y

  competència, impulsa millores i extres, envia a cada hoste ofertes a la seva
  mida i et prepara un resum cada matí. Sobre el PMS que ja fas servir.
