# Fondas — Go-to-Market Plan

_Created 27 July 2026. Consolidates `PILOT_OUTREACH.md`, the commercial half of `LAUNCH_PLAN.md`, and the market sections of `ROADMAP.md` / `MARKET_STRATEGY.md`. Product and engineering work lives separately in `BUILD_PLAN_JULY31.md`._

**Starting position: zero.** No hotels identified, no messages sent, no tracking sheet, no legal entity. This plan starts from actual nothing on **Saturday 1 August** and is dated accordingly.

---

## 1. The honest calendar problem

The single most important scheduling fact in this document:

> **August is the worst month of the year to cold-contact Spanish hotel GMs — and it's the month you're starting in.**

Coastal properties (Costa Brava, Costa Daurada, the islands) run 90–100% occupancy through August. Their GMs are working 12-hour days. City hotels in Barcelona and Madrid are busy but marginally more reachable. Meanwhile much of Spanish business life slows to a crawl for the second half of the month.

This does **not** mean waiting. It means calibrating expectations and sequencing correctly:

| Window | What it's good for | Expected response rate |
|---|---|---|
| **Aug 3–21** | List building, first touches, warm intros, a few opportunistic demos | Low — 5–10% |
| **Aug 24 – Sep 4** | Season winding down; GMs surface. Demos start landing | Moderate |
| **Sep 7 – Oct 15** | **The real window.** Post-season reviews, budget planning for next year, appetite for new tools | Best — this is when to push hardest |

**Strategic consequence:** target **city hotels (Barcelona, Madrid)** in August, and hold the **coastal properties (Costa Brava, Costa Daurada)** for September, when they're doing post-season reviews and planning next season. Contacting a Costa Brava GM on 12 August is burning a good lead at the worst possible moment — and in a small industry, a badly timed first impression is expensive.

## 2. Goal and funnel math

**Primary goal:** 3 hotels using Fondas daily, with at least one saying yes to €199/month.

Funnel from `PILOT_OUTREACH.md` §8, which holds up:

```
40 hotels identified
 → 25 contacted
   → 10 reply
     → 6 take a demo
       → 3 onboard as pilots
         → 1+ converts to paying
```

That's ~12% contact-to-pilot, realistic for warm, personalised outreach to a tight ICP. Identify 40 rather than 30 to absorb August's lower response rate.

**Cadence:** 5 contacts/day, 5 days/week. 25 contacts = one week of sending, plus follow-ups trailing for three weeks after.

## 3. Who you're targeting

| Signal | Why |
|---|---|
| **20–80 rooms** | Small enough that the GM does their own morning prep and email; big enough to feel overwhelmed |
| **Independent or 1–3 properties** | No procurement, no IT department. The GM or owner decides |
| **Uses MEWS** | The only PMS wired end-to-end today. **Apaleo is not connected yet** — don't promise it |
| **Uses Gmail for operations** | The email assistant is Gmail-only |
| **4-star or design-forward** | These GMs care about output quality; a beautiful brief lands |
| **City or urban boutique** | Busier daily ops. Prioritise in August |

**Disqualifiers:** chain-affiliated (Marriott/IHG/Accor) · Opera/Cloudbeds/other PMS · Outlook or shared non-Gmail inbox · under 15 rooms.

### Geography sequencing

- **August:** Barcelona city, Madrid city. Dense, reachable, English- and Spanish-speaking, high MEWS adoption.
- **September onward:** Costa Brava, Costa Daurada, Sitges, Girona — plus Valencia, Seville, Bilbao, San Sebastián. Coastal properties are ideal ICP but only reachable post-season.
- **Advantage to use:** you're in Barcelona. Offer to visit in person. For a Costa Brava property in September, "I can drive up Tuesday" converts far better than a Zoom link.

## 4. Week 1 (Mon 3 – Fri 7 Aug) — build the list

**Deliverable: 40 qualified hotels in a tracking sheet, 15 contacted.**

Channels, in order of lead quality:

1. **Warm network first.** Before any cold outreach: does anyone you know work in hospitality, hotel consulting, F&B, or hospitality tech? One warm intro beats 20 cold messages. Spend the first hour here.
2. **MEWS ecosystem** — MEWS publishes case studies and a marketplace; hotels featured there are confirmed users. LinkedIn search `"Mews" "General Manager" Barcelona` surfaces GMs who name their PMS publicly.
3. **Design collections** — Design Hotels, Small Luxury Hotels, Mr & Mrs Smith, Tablet Hotels. All have public, searchable member directories. Filter by Spain, then by size.
4. **LinkedIn direct** — `"General Manager" "boutique hotel" Barcelona` / `Madrid`. Prioritise 2nd-degree connections.
5. **Instagram** — boutique hotels are obsessive about it. `#boutiquehotelbarcelona`, `#hotelboutiquemadrid`. DMs are often read by the owner directly, especially for smaller properties.

**Tracking sheet columns:**

`Hotel · City · Rooms · PMS (confirmed?) · GM name · Source · Date contacted · Status · Last touch · Next action · Notes`

Status values: `found → contacted → replied → demo booked → demo done → onboarding → active pilot → paying` (or `dead`).

## 5. The outreach sequence

Four touches maximum, then stop. In a small industry, a fifth follow-up costs you reputation.

```
Day 1   →  LinkedIn connection request (with note)
Day 4   →  LinkedIn message (the actual ask)
Day 11  →  Email follow-up
Day 18  →  Final note, with the sample-brief offer
```

**Write in Spanish to Spanish GMs.** English-first outreach to a Barcelona or Girona property signals you're not local — and being local is one of your few structural advantages against Otel AI, Altek, and the rest. Both versions below.

### Touch 1 — LinkedIn connection request (300 char limit)

**ES:**

**EN:**
> Hi [Name] — I'm building an operations tool for boutique hotels and would love to connect. We're launching with 3 pilot hotels and [Hotel] looks like exactly the kind of property we had in mind.

**ES:**

>

## 6. The demo (20 minutes)

Goal is not to sell. It's to make them feel smart for taking the call.

| Min | What you do |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Ask about their morning. *"¿Cómo es tu primera hora? ¿Dónde se te va más tiempo antes de que llegue el equipo?"* Let them describe the pain first. |
| 3–6 | **Open on the inbox** — Concierge with a real draft waiting. This is the differentiated moment; don't bury it. |
| 6–12 | Then the brief. Read part of it aloud. Then check-in chasing, then ask-anything chat. Narrate the day, not the features: *"Son las 6:45, abres esto…"* |
| 12–14 | The fragmentation close: *"Podrías pagar seis suscripciones — comunicaciones, revenue, finanzas, tareas, experiencias, CRM — o una capa que te lleva la mañana."* |
| 14–17 | *"¿Esto se parece a algo que te ahorraría tiempo?"* Then stop talking. |
| 17–20 | If yes: *"El siguiente paso es conectar tu PMS, son 30 minutos y lo hacemos juntos. ¿Lo agendamos?"* |

**Never say "AI" during the demo.** Say *"Fondas lee tus datos y escribe esto."* Saying AI makes them think ChatGPT and raises their guard. This is already your design principle — hold it.

**Before every demo:** add their Google account as a **Test user** in the OAuth consent screen the day the demo is booked, not the day of onboarding. Google verification isn't submitted (see §8), so without this their Gmail connect will fail in front of them.

## 8. Legal — where you actually stand

You have no legal entity yet, and you're piloting first to test fit before incorporating. That's a defensible sequence, but be clear-eyed about what it does and doesn't allow.

**What's fine:** running free pilots as an individual. No billing, no invoices, no company needed.

**What's genuinely at risk:** the moment a hotel's guest data flows through Fondas, GDPR applies regardless of whether you've incorporated. The hotel is the data controller, you're the processor, and Article 28 expects a written processing agreement between them. A well-run 4-star property with a competent DPO may ask for a DPA before connecting Gmail. Some won't ask at all — but you should expect the question, and having no answer will cost you a good lead.

**Practical approach for the pilot phase:**

1. **Write a plain one-page pilot agreement** covering: what data is accessed (PMS reservations, Gmail messages), what it's used for, that it isn't used to train models, where it's stored (Supabase EU), sub-processors (Supabase, Anthropic, Google, Resend, Vercel, Sentry), and that the hotel can terminate and have data deleted on request. Not a substitute for a lawyer-drafted DPA, but far better than nothing, and it answers the question honestly.
2. **Fill `company.ts` with what's true today** rather than leaving `[Fondas Technologies, S.L.]` placeholders on a live site. Placeholder legal pages on a public domain look worse than modest ones. If you're operating as an individual, say so.
3. **Don't submit Google OAuth verification yet** — it wants a real entity and privacy policy, and pilots work fine as test users. This is correctly deferred.

**Hard triggers — when this stops being optional:**

| Trigger | What you must have first |
|---|---|
| Charging anyone | Legal entity (SL or autónomo), invoicing, Stripe |
| A hotel's DPO asks for a DPA | Lawyer-drafted DPA |
| Opening public signups | Entity + Google verification + real legal pages |
| More than ~3 pilots | Entity — informal arrangements stop scaling |

**Recommendation:** book the lawyer conversation the week your **second** pilot goes live (around 4 September), not after the third. Spanish SL incorporation takes a few weeks; starting it in October means charging in November.

_Not legal advice — I'm not a lawyer, and Spanish/EU data protection is exactly the area where a real one is worth the money._

## 9. Pilot onboarding runbook

When a demo converts, book a **30-minute screen-share**. Do these in order:

1. **Before the call:** add their Google account as an OAuth test user. Confirm they're on MEWS and Gmail.
2. Create their account → onboarding wizard → connect MEWS → first sync (this is B10's job; if it's not shipped, do it via Settings)
3. Generate a **preview brief live on the call** — this is the moment they get it. Don't skip it.
4. Connect Gmail
5. Fill **Hotel Profile & Tone together** — this is where the product gets good, and it's much better done conversationally than left as homework. Include the **upsell fields** (late checkout, breakfast, transfer, parking with prices — B6 shipped this) so the chat can already answer guest questions correctly.
6. Set **brief recipients** (their email), **send hour**, and **language** — Spanish for Spanish GMs. Getting an English brief kills it.
7. Open a **WhatsApp thread** with them before ending the call. Not email — WhatsApp. Response rates are incomparable, and it's how you'll find out something broke.

**Then, daily for the first week:** read their brief yourself every morning before they do. Fix what's wrong before they report it. This is the highest-value hour of the whole pilot programme.

## 10. During the pilot — what to measure

From `MARKET_STRATEGY.md` §3.6, the metrics that indicate fit:

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Draft acceptance rate** (sent unedited / minor edit / major edit / discarded) | The #1 PMF signal. Needs B12 + B16 to measure properly; until then, ask them directly |
| Brief opens, and whether they read it before arriving | Is it actually part of the morning? |
| Chat queries per week | Are they treating it as the place to ask? |
| Unprompted feature requests | Strongest possible signal — they're imagining it in their workflow |

**Structured feedback at 2 weeks**, per pilot (15 min or 5 written questions):

1. What's wrong or missing in the brief?
2. Which drafts did you edit, and why?
3. What do you now not do, that you used to do?
4. What would make you stop using it?
5. What would you pay for it?

Paste verbatim answers into `PILOT_FEEDBACK.md` — that file drives the B14 fix cycle and becomes case-study raw material (ask permission to quote).

## 11. Converting to paid

**Timing:** ask at week 2–3 of an active pilot, not later. Momentum decays.

**The ask** — direct, no discount theatre:

> "Llevas tres semanas usándolo. ¿Te sirve lo suficiente como para que valga 199€ al mes? Si la respuesta es no, dime qué falta — es igual de útil para mí."

**Pricing:** hold **€199/month flat per property.** The independent band is €99–300, often €4/room. Flat beats per-room above ~50 rooms and is easier to say yes to. Don't discount the first customer — discount the *terms* instead (monthly, cancel anytime, no setup fee) if you need to reduce friction.

**Billing rollout** (engineering, August–September, tracked in the build plan not here):

| Item | When |
|---|---|
| Legal entity + bank account | Trigger: 2nd pilot live (~4 Sep) |
| Stripe: plan, checkout, portal, trial gating | Before the first ask (~late Sep) |
| Rate limiting + per-hotel spend caps | Before public signups |
| Real legal pages + DPA | Before charging |

## 13. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **August response rates are poor** | Expected and planned for. Judge the funnel in September, not on 20 August. Don't panic-discount or widen the ICP because week 2 was quiet. |
| **Burning coastal leads by contacting them in peak season** | Hold Costa Brava / Costa Daurada until September. Deliberate, not lazy. |
| **A pilot asks for a DPA you don't have** | The §8 one-page agreement covers most cases. If a property insists, be honest that the formal DPA is weeks away, and offer to start in September — better than bluffing. |
| **Gmail OAuth fails live in a demo** | Add every prospect as a test user the day the demo is booked. Test the flow yourself once a week. |
| **MEWS-only limits the funnel** | Real constraint. If ≥3 good prospects are on Apaleo, wiring it becomes the top August build task — Apaleo credentials are currently empty. |
| **First pilot has a bad first week** | You read their brief every morning before they do, for the first week. A broken pilot is worse than a late one. |
| **Sandbox-quality demo data** | Before demos start, seed the test hotel with realistic Spanish guest names and a plausible occupancy pattern. Half an hour, and it makes the demo feel real. |
