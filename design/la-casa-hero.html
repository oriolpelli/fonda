La Casa, three views.

One hotel — the same cream façade, terracotta roof, arcaded loggia and corner tower — drawn three ways. Water is in all three, as you asked: a still pool, an infinity edge above the sea, and a sliver of blue at the arrival corner. Detail sits in the architecture (arches, balustrades, lanterns, shutters); everything else stays a loose wash so the illustration supports the headline instead of competing with it.

On the medium

These are drawn in the browser, so the washes are approximations of a brush — they lock the scene, palette and crop. Each view ships with a paint brief you can hand to an illustrator or paste into an image tool for the final 2×-width artwork.

1a

La Piscina — the still pool

Your favourite reference, developed: the full symmetrical façade, five-arch loggia with a lantern-lit centre door, first-floor balustrade, and a corner tower — reflected in a quiet pool with a patterned terracotta deck. The most "this is a real, beautiful hotel" of the three.

Delicate watercolour and fine ink illustration of a two-storey Mediterranean boutique hotel, seen straight on. Cream stucco façade; ground-floor loggia of five round arches with a warm wooden door at the centre and stone steps; first floor of five tall arched windows with pale blue-grey shutters and a continuous blue-grey stone balustrade; terracotta pantile roof; a taller corner tower on the right with a pyramid tile roof and two arched openings; wrought-iron wall lanterns; clipped shrubs and two potted olives. Plain white paper background, nothing behind the building, generous negative space at the top. Elegant, airy, hand-painted. No text.

Vora Mar — the same house, by the sea

The same building pulled back onto a headland: an infinity pool in the foreground, open blue bay behind, two cypresses, a far village on the point, palms at the right edge. Wider and calmer — the most "boutique hotel somewhere you want to be", and the most negative space for a headline.

Where I'd land

1a as the hero. It's the closest to the reference you liked, it's symmetrical enough to sit under a centred headline, and the pool gives you the water without adding a whole landscape. Paint it at 2× hero width with real paper texture.

1b as the second surface — the CTA band or the "about" page, where a wider, quieter piece works. 1c for the pieces: the lantern, the olive pot and the arch pulled out as small spot vignettes for feature rows, exactly like the Pillows sheet.

One rule for whichever you pick: the art stays under 40% of the hero's height, never behind the type, and never more than one illustration per screen. It's the warm layer — the words still do the work.
